How to start a private-label underwear brand in 7 steps

Most new underwear brands do not fail on design. They fail on vague briefs, skipped samples and labels that do not meet import rules. This is the order we recommend to buyers who produce with us for the first time.

Published 2026-11-21

1. Decide who you sell to and at what price

Start with one customer and one price point, not a full collection. A brand selling everyday men's trunks in supermarkets needs a different fabric, packaging and cost than a premium women's line sold online. Fabric is the biggest cost driver in underwear, so your retail price largely decides which fibers you can use: combed or Supima cotton and modal for everyday lines, mulberry silk or fine 80S rib for premium ones.

Two or three styles in three or four colors is a realistic first range. You can widen it once you know which sizes and colors sell.

2. Choose OEM, ODM or in-stock

There are three ways to work with a factory, and they differ mainly in time and development work:

  • OEM: the factory produces your own design from your tech pack. Best when you already have a proven fit.
  • ODM: you start from a style the factory has already developed and change the fabric, color, waistband, labels and packaging. Faster, because the pattern is already tested.
  • In-stock with your label: existing styles in stock, with your labels and packaging added. The quickest way to test a market.

Our guide OEM vs ODM underwear compares them in detail.

3. Write a clear brief or tech pack

The quality of the quote depends on the quality of the brief. Even for ODM, send:

  • the style (a sketch, a photo, a reference sample or one of our style codes such as M-SB01 or ZY05)
  • a size chart with measurements, and the market you sell in (US, EU, Latin American or Asian sizing differ)
  • fabric composition and weight, or the hand-feel you want
  • colors as Pantone references
  • waistband, label and packaging artwork
  • quantity per style and per color, target delivery date and target price

4. Approve fabrics and colors first

Ask for fabric swatches and lab dips before any garment sample. It is much cheaper to change a fabric or a shade at this stage than after sampling. Check stretch and recovery, opacity in light colors, and how the fabric feels after a wash.

5. Sample twice: fit, then pre-production

The fit sample confirms the pattern and sizing; try it on real people in at least two sizes. The pre-production (PP) sample is made in the final fabric, with your waistband and labels, and becomes the reference for bulk production and inspection. Do not start bulk without signing off the PP sample.

6. Bulk production and quality control

With us, bulk production typically takes 35 days after PP approval and the deposit. During production, goods are inspected in line and again at the end at AQL 2.5, the common standard for apparel. Ask for photos during production, and consider a third-party inspection before shipment for your first order.

7. Labels, packing and shipping

Underwear labels are regulated in most markets. In the United States, labels must show fiber content, country of origin and the identity of the manufacturer, importer or dealer, and a care label is also required. Mexico applies its own textile labeling standard (NOM-004), and other Latin American countries have similar rules, usually in Spanish. Give the factory the exact wording for your market before printing labels.

Decide on the shipping term early: with FOB you book the freight from the Chinese port; with CIF the factory side books it to your port. Cartons are packed to your packing list and carton marks.

Common first-order mistakes

  • Too many styles and colors, so each one falls below an efficient production quantity.
  • A size chart copied from another brand without checking it on real customers.
  • Approving bulk from photos instead of a physical PP sample.
  • Printing labels and packaging before confirming the legal text for the destination country.
